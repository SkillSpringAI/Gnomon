# Gnomon Epistemic and Evidence Authority

## Purpose and status

This document is the maintained Markdown form of Gnomon’s epistemic and evidence authority. It defines how Gnomon represents, evaluates, relates, and communicates knowledge without collapsing fundamentally different epistemic objects into one undifferentiated category of information.

These rules are normative. The implementation-status section identifies what the current repository demonstrates and what remains partial or deferred. It does not weaken any requirement.

## Foundational chain

Gnomon treats knowledge as a structured, evolving body of evidence:

```text
Source
  -> Observation
  -> Claim
  -> Evidence relationship
  -> Assessment
  -> Inference
  -> Hypothesis
  -> Conclusion
```

Not every item passes through every stage. When a stage exists, it must remain distinguishable from the others. A source is not a claim, a claim is not a fact, an assessment is not an eternal truth, and an inference is not a direct observation.

## Epistemic objects

| Object | Meaning | Required distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Origin from which information was obtained | Origin does not automatically imply truth. |
| Observation | What Gnomon encountered or recorded | Does not automatically assert that the content is true. |
| Evidence | Information relevant to evaluating a question, claim, or hypothesis | Retains provenance and may support or contradict. |
| Claim | Proposition that can be evaluated against evidence | Existence in storage does not establish truth. |
| Hypothesis | Proposition under investigation | Is not a fact, established claim, or conclusion by default. |
| Hypothesis assessment | Current evaluation of evidence relating to a hypothesis | Is revisable research state, not eternal truth. |
| Inference | Proposition generated by reasoning over observations, claims, or evidence | Remains distinguishable from direct observation and retains dependencies. |
| Conclusion | Research-level synthesis of the current evidence state | Remains traceable to supporting evidence and assessments. |
| Agent observation | Information received through an external-agent boundary | Is agent-sourced evidence, not automatic authoritative knowledge. |

## Source and provenance

A source may be a webpage, paper, dataset, user document, API response, or external-agent response. Where available, a source retains its type, identifier or URI, publisher or author, publication and retrieval times, content hash, version, originating agent, access metadata, reliability metadata, and provenance relationships.

Every accepted substantive claim retains meaningful provenance identifying where it came from, what evidence generated or supported it, whether it was extracted, supplied, or inferred, and when it was created. Provenance should also preserve relationships to supporting and contradicting evidence.

Knowledge without traceable origin must not silently replace traceable knowledge.

## Claims, assessments, and uncertainty

A claim is intentionally neutral. It may be unverified, weakly supported, supported, strongly supported, disputed, contradicted, superseded, retracted, or unresolved. A claim’s presence in a database must never be presented as proof of truth.

Confidence represents a current, attributable, timestamped, revisable assessment. It is not the same as truth, evidence quality, or source reliability. Source reliability and claim confidence remain separate properties.

An assessment should account for supporting, contradicting, and unresolved evidence, source independence, temporal relevance, methodological limitations, alternative explanations, and remaining uncertainty. When evidence conflicts, Gnomon preserves the disagreement until an explicit assessment justifies a change. Majority vote, recency, model confidence, or familiarity with an agent is not sufficient conflict resolution.

Reports should distinguish observations, supported claims, contested claims, inferences, hypotheses, unresolved questions, limitations, and provenance. They must not present model-generated synthesis as direct source evidence.

## Corroboration and independence

Corroboration requires substantially independent evidence. Repetition is not corroboration: several agents or documents may merely copy one upstream source. Where possible, the system should distinguish independent, derived, common-source, and unknown-dependency relationships.

Agent reputation can influence evidence evaluation but never grants policy, security, permission, memory-write, or research-rule authority. Agent recommendations are initially research leads, criticism, candidate evidence, or alternative hypotheses and should be independently evaluated where material.

## Temporal and contextual integrity

Evidence can become stale without becoming false. Sources and claims should retain relevant dates, temporal scope, population, geography, measurement, methodology, qualifiers, and other context necessary to avoid misleading interpretation.

Quantitative evidence should preserve value, unit, denominator, timeframe, population, geography, methodology, uncertainty, and source where applicable. Correlation must not be silently promoted to causation. Forecasts and predictions remain separate from observations and historical claims, and historical predictions must not be rewritten after outcomes are known.

## Retraction, dependency, and reassessment

When evidence is retracted or invalidated, Gnomon must not silently erase dependent knowledge. It should identify affected claims, assessments, inferences, conclusions, and reports for reassessment. New evidence should update current assessment while preserving the historical state that explains why earlier conclusions were reached.

Historical epistemic states must not be rewritten to imply that later evidence was available earlier. Research workflow state is separate from epistemic state: an investigation may be concluded while its claims remain uncertain or contested.

## Research planning implications

Stopping criteria distinguish between having answered a question sufficiently, exhausting available resources, and being unable to investigate further. Meaningful evidence gaps include unsearched populations or time periods, missing geographic coverage, weak source classes, unresolved contradictions, and hypotheses lacking direct evidence.

More evidence is not necessarily better evidence. Planning should favor source diversity, independence, contradiction hunting, methodological quality, and unresolved questions over repeated near-identical material. Where appropriate, research should actively seek counterexamples and alternative explanations.

Semantic similarity and embeddings may help discover relevant information. They are not evidence of truth, causality, source reliability, or corroboration, and they must not become the only retained representation of important knowledge.

## Mandatory epistemic invariants

1. Every accepted substantive claim has provenance.
2. A claim is not automatically a fact.
3. Source reliability and claim confidence are distinct.
4. Agent reputation is distinct from system authority.
5. Repetition is not automatically corroboration.
6. Model agreement is not independent empirical evidence.
7. Contradictory evidence remains representable.
8. Inferences retain dependencies upon their inputs.
9. Historical epistemic states are not silently rewritten.
10. Retrieved information cannot redefine system instructions.
11. Uncertainty remains representable.
12. Absence of evidence does not automatically become evidence of absence.
13. Vector similarity is not evidence of truth.
14. Important conclusions remain traceable to supporting evidence.
15. Retraction permits dependent knowledge to be identified for reassessment.

## Epistemic integrity test

For any substantive conclusion, Gnomon should be able to answer:

- What exactly is being asserted?
- Is it an observation, claim, inference, hypothesis, assessment, or conclusion?
- Where did it originate?
- What evidence supports and contradicts it?
- How independent and reliable are the supporting sources?
- What assumptions, context, and temporal limits apply?
- What uncertainty remains?
- Has the information become stale or has supporting evidence been retracted?
- What would cause the current assessment to change?

If these questions cannot be answered adequately, the report should communicate the resulting uncertainty.

## Current implementation status

| Requirement area | Current evidence and limitation |
|---|---|
| Sources, evidence, claims, provenance, and hypothesis assessments | Implemented in the current research and persistence paths with source hashes, provenance links, assessments, and uncertainty-aware reports. |
| Agent observations | Implemented as bounded agent-message evidence with task-scoped provenance and derived comparison metadata; live external networks remain absent. |
| Governed assessment history | Current claims and hypothesis assessments use versioned governed memory and append-only change history; broader dependent reassessment propagation remains open. |
| Contradiction and corroboration semantics | Current reports preserve deterministic agreement, contradiction, duplicate, and distinct-agent metadata; full source-dependency graphs and domain-specific evidence weighting remain partial. |
| Historical epistemic preservation | Existing governed memory history preserves accepted changes, but the full authority requirement for all historical assessment transitions remains a release concern. |
| Temporal validity, forecasts, causal claims, and rich context | Partially represented or deferred; do not infer full support from the current claim and assessment model. |
| Semantic/vector retrieval and embeddings | Deferred; structured evidence and provenance remain authoritative. |
